Why DevOps?

Initially, software was developed using the Waterfall model.

Waterfall Focus:
- Development
- Implementation
- Delivery

Once the product was delivered, the development team's job was considered complete.

Problems with Waterfall:
- Difficult to handle requirement changes.
- Poor collaboration.
- Slow feedback.
- Customer issues were addressed only after delivery.
- Changes required significant effort.

To overcome these problems, the industry adopted Agile.

Agile Improvements:
- Faster development
- Better customer collaboration
- Frequent releases
- Flexibility for changing requirements
- Continuous feedback

Even Agile had one limitation.

Agile mainly focused on:
- Development
- Testing
- Delivery

After deployment, developers usually had very little involvement.

Who actually uses the software?

Example:
A banking application is delivered to a bank.

Users:
- Bank employees
- Operational staff
- End customers

These users may face:
- Bugs
- Performance issues
- Configuration problems
- Production errors

The Operations team manages these issues.

This created a gap between:
- Development Team
- Operations Team

To bridge this gap, DevOps was introduced.

DevOps = Development + Operations

DevOps is a culture and set of practices where Development and Operations teams work together throughout the software lifecycle.

DevOps Lifecycle:

Requirements
      |
      V
Development
      |
      V
Testing
      |
      V
Deployment
      |
      V
Operations
      |
      V
Monitoring
      |
      V
Customer Feedback
      |
      V
Continuous Improvement

Benefits of DevOps:
- Better collaboration
- Faster software delivery
- Continuous Integration (CI)
- Continuous Delivery/Deployment (CD)
- Faster issue resolution
- Better monitoring
- Higher software quality
- Improved customer satisfaction

Key Point:
Traditional approach = Develop → Test → Deliver

DevOps approach = Develop → Test → Deploy → Operate → Monitor → Improve continuously
